Unit 12

What will be the most influential trend in SRM in the next 5 years?

This unit explored emerging and influential trends in security and risk management, particularly the growing role of artificial intelligence, cyber resilience, identity security, post-quantum cryptography, third-party risk and the legal, ethical, social and professional challenges facing organisations. It also consolidated the risk-management methodologies, tools and techniques studied throughout the module.

My Objectives:

My objective was to understand how emerging technologies are changing the security risk landscape and to consider how organisations can adapt existing governance and risk-management practices to address these changes. I also wanted to consolidate the knowledge developed throughout the module and consider how it relates to my professional experience in enterprise IT and security.

Unit 12

Tasks Done

I reviewed the Unit 12 materials and final seminar on emerging security and risk-management trends. I examined Agentic AI, identity and access management, cyber resilience, post-quantum cryptography, human and third-party risk, and the associated legal, ethical and professional considerations. I also revisited methodologies and techniques covered earlier in the module, including FAIR, NIST CSF, ISO 27001, Monte Carlo simulation, attack-path analysis and control-effectiveness measurement.

Reflection

Unit 12 Reflection

This unit brought together many of the concepts studied throughout the module and showed me that security risk management is increasingly about resilience rather than prevention alone. I found the discussion of Agentic AI particularly relevant because autonomous systems introduce identities, permissions and actions that must be governed alongside human accounts. This reinforced my understanding that established practices such as least privilege, Zero Trust, monitoring and strong identity governance will remain important, but must evolve to accommodate non-human identities. I also found the discussion of control effectiveness valuable. Implementing a security control does not necessarily demonstrate that risk has been adequately reduced. Measuring the effect of controls and reassessing residual risk provides stronger evidence for security investment decisions. This connects well with my professional experience because enterprise security requires a balance between implementing technical controls, maintaining operational resilience and demonstrating that those controls provide meaningful risk reduction.

Outcome

Unit Outcome

I developed a broader understanding of how emerging technologies, particularly Agentic AI, are reshaping security risk management. I also consolidated my understanding of the frameworks and quantitative approaches covered throughout the module and recognised the importance of combining risk assessment, identity governance, continuous monitoring, control effectiveness and organisational resilience rather than relying solely on preventative security measures
