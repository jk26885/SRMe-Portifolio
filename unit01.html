Unit 1

An Introduction to Security and Risk Management

In week one we reviewed some common definitions of, and approaches to, Security and Risk Management.

Unit Objectives

  • Review a number of common definitions of Security and Risk Management.
  • Review some common Security and Risk Management concepts.
  • Discuss the Risk Management Process.

Objectives: The unit aimed to develop a foundational understanding of security and risk management by:

  • Reviewing common definitions of security and risk management.
  • Examining key security and risk management concepts.
  • Understanding the risk management process.
  • Exploring approaches used to assess, quantify, qualify and mitigate risks.
  • Identifying common risk management standards and frameworks.
Unit 1

Tasks Done

  • Reviewed key concepts of risk science, information risk management and security risk management, including the role of uncertainty in decision-making, the value of information as an organisational asset, and the relationship between security, governance and organisational objectives.
  • Examined structured risk management processes involving threat, vulnerability, impact and control analysis, alongside approaches for risk assessment, mitigation and continuous monitoring.
  • Critically evaluated traditional and modern risk management practices, including qualitative and quantitative assessment methods, the limitations of subjective risk scoring, and the importance of evidence-based decision-making.
  • Explored emerging risk factors such as digital transformation, remote working and evolving technological dependencies, and their implications for organisational resilience, business continuity and compliance.
Reflection

Unit 1 Reflection

This unit provided a strong foundation for understanding security and risk management and highlighted that risk management is fundamentally about making informed decisions under uncertainty. Prior to this unit, I largely associated risk management with identifying threats and implementing controls. However, the readings demonstrated that risk management is a broader discipline that incorporates governance, communication, organisational context and the quality of available knowledge. One of the most valuable insights came from Aven and Thekdi's discussion of uncertainty. I learned that risk cannot always be expressed through probability alone and that decision-makers must consider the strength and limitations of the information used when assessing risk. This perspective aligns closely with real-world cybersecurity environments, where decisions often need to be made despite incomplete information. The NIST SP 800-30 framework provided a practical methodology for conducting risk assessments. Its structured approach to identifying threats, vulnerabilities, impacts and controls reinforced the importance of using a systematic process rather than relying on assumptions or intuition. As an IT professional, I was able to relate this framework to security assessments and infrastructure projects I have been involved with, where understanding business impact is as important as understanding technical vulnerabilities. Hubbard's critique of traditional risk management approaches challenged some of my existing assumptions. His argument that qualitative ratings such as "high", "medium" and "low" can lead to inconsistent decision-making highlighted the importance of quantitative analysis where appropriate. This was particularly relevant to cybersecurity risk management, where subjective assessments can sometimes oversimplify complex risks. Sutton's discussion of information risk management reinforced the idea that information is one of an organisation's most valuable assets. The chapter demonstrated that effective risk management is not solely a technical activity but also a governance responsibility that supports operational resilience, compliance and organisational objectives.

Outcome

Unit Outcome

Overall, this unit successfully established the theoretical and practical foundations required for future study in security and risk management. It enhanced my understanding of how risks are identified, assessed, communicated and mitigated, while also highlighting the importance of selecting appropriate frameworks and methodologies based on organisational needs. The knowledge gained during this unit will support my future exploration of risk assessment techniques, cybersecurity governance and information security management practices.
