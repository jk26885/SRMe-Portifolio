Unit 2

Users, Assessments and the Risk Management Process

In uint 2 I conduct a deeper dive into the Risk Management Process, specifically looking at the effect of different types of assessment (Qualitative vs. Quantitative) and the role and importance of user participation in the process.

Unit Objectives

  • Review the Risk Management Process (RMP).
  • Review the effects of different assessment types (Qualitative vs. Quantitative).
  • Discuss potential changes to the RMP based on changes in user participation.

Tasks Completed:

  • Reviewed the Risk Management Process (RMP) and examined how risk identification, assessment, treatment, monitoring and governance activities support organisational decision-making.
  • Studied both qualitative and quantitative risk assessment approaches, including the Open FAIR methodology, and evaluated their strengths, limitations and suitability for different risk scenarios.
  • Explored secure systems engineering principles, focusing on the relationship between threats, vulnerabilities, adversaries and countermeasures throughout the system lifecycle.
  • Examined Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) frameworks and ISO 31000 principles, including the integration of risk management into organisational strategy, governance and operations.
  • Investigated the importance of stakeholder and user participation in risk management, particularly in identifying risks, evaluating controls and determining acceptable risk levels.
  • Analysed the implications of risk mitigation decisions, including business, operational, financial and usability considerations.
  • Explored the role of emerging technologies, digitalisation and artificial intelligence in shaping organisational risk profiles and influencing future risk management practices.
Unit 2

Achievements: By completing the unit readings, seminar preparation and collaborative activities, I was able to::

  • Differentiate between qualitative and quantitative risk assessment methodologies and identify situations where each approach may be most appropriate.
  • Develop an understanding of quantitative risk analysis techniques such as Open FAIR and their role in producing measurable, evidence-based risk assessments.
  • Recognise the importance of user and stakeholder participation throughout the risk management process to improve risk identification, ownership and decision-making.
  • Explain how security requirements should be integrated into system design and development rather than treated as an afterthought.
  • Understand how Enterprise Risk Management supports organisational objectives through improved governance, accountability and informed decision-making.
  • Evaluate the potential benefits and consequences of recommended risk mitigation measures from both technical and business perspectives.
  • Appreciate the opportunities and risks associated with digital transformation, including the need to balance innovation, security, sustainability and organisational resilience.
Reflection

Unit 2 Reflection

This unit expanded my understanding of risk management beyond the assessment of threats and vulnerabilities by highlighting the importance of assessment methodologies and stakeholder involvement. One of the key lessons was that the quality of risk management outcomes is heavily influenced by how risks are assessed and who participates in the process. The comparison between qualitative and quantitative assessment approaches was particularly valuable. Prior to this unit, I was familiar with the widespread use of qualitative methods that classify risks as high, medium or low. However, studying the Open FAIR framework demonstrated how quantitative approaches can provide more defensible and measurable estimates of risk exposure, allowing organisations to make decisions based on data rather than subjective judgement. At the same time, I recognised that qualitative approaches remain useful where sufficient data is unavailable or when rapid assessments are required. The secure systems engineering methodology reinforced the importance of considering security from the earliest stages of system design. Rather than addressing security issues after implementation, the approach emphasises understanding adversaries, identifying vulnerabilities and selecting cost-effective countermeasures that align with operational requirements. This concept aligns closely with modern cybersecurity best practices and my professional experience in infrastructure and cloud security projects. Another important insight was the role of user participation in the Risk Management Process. Effective risk management cannot be performed solely by security professionals. System owners, business users and other stakeholders possess valuable knowledge regarding business processes, operational requirements and risk tolerance. Their involvement improves the accuracy of risk assessments and helps ensure that mitigation measures are practical and aligned with organisational objectives. The discussion on digitalisation and emerging technologies also highlighted the increasingly complex nature of risk management. While technologies such as artificial intelligence and digital transformation initiatives can improve efficiency and innovation, they also introduce new security, privacy and governance challenges. This reinforced the need for a balanced, enterprise-wide approach that considers both opportunities and risks when making decisions. Overall, this unit strengthened my understanding of how risk assessment methodologies, stakeholder engagement and governance frameworks contribute to effective risk management. It also demonstrated the importance of selecting appropriate assessment techniques and ensuring that mitigation strategies support both security objectives and business outcomes.

Outcome

Unit Outcome

Unit 2 provided a deeper understanding of the Risk Management Process by examining the differences between qualitative and quantitative assessment methods and the importance of user participation. Through the study of Open FAIR, secure systems engineering principles, Enterprise Risk Management frameworks and the risks associated with digitalisation, I developed a broader perspective on how organisations identify, assess and manage risk. The unit demonstrated that effective risk management requires a combination of structured methodologies, stakeholder involvement, evidence-based analysis and continuous review. It also highlighted that recommended mitigations must be evaluated not only from a security perspective but also in terms of their operational, financial and organisational impact. These lessons provide a solid foundation for further study of risk assessment techniques, cybersecurity governance and information security management.
