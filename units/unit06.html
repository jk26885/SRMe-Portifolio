Unit 6

The Practical Implications of Security and Risk Standards

This week focused on understanding how legal, regulatory and industry standards contribute to security and risk management. The unit focused particularly on GDPR, relevant industry standards such as PCI DSS, and the selection of appropriate controls to reduce organisational risk

My objectives were to:

  • Understand how GDPR applies to different business situations.
  • Assess security risks, threats and vulnerabilities using suitable methodologies.
  • Recommend proportionate technical, organisational and procedural controls.
  • Understand how compliance and standards support wider Security and Risk Management strategies.
Unit 6

Tasks Done

I reviewed GDPR scenarios and considered how requirements for protecting personal information apply in practical business situations. I also examined industry standards such as PCI DSS and considered how organisations can identify the standards and controls relevant to their activities. Reading, Sørensen's (2018) Digitalisation: An Opportunity or a Risk?, encouraged me to consider digital transformation from both opportunity and risk perspectives. Rather than assuming that digitalisation is inherently beneficial, I considered how organisations must balance potential innovation and efficiency against new risks and regulatory challenges.

Reflection

Unit 6 Reflection

The most significant lesson from Unit 6 was that regulations and standards establish important requirements, but organisations still need to understand their particular business context and risks before deciding which controls are appropriate.The unit also developed my understanding of security frameworks. I previously tended to approach security primarily from a technical perspective because of my professional experience. I now see more clearly that selecting controls should follow an assessment of business objectives, information assets, threats, regulatory obligations and risk tolerance. GDPR provided a useful example of this relationship. Compliance is not achieved simply by deploying security technologies. Effective protection also requires appropriate organisational processes concerning how information is collected, accessed, retained, shared and eventually removed.

Outcome

Unit Outcome

I developed a stronger understanding of how security frameworks, standards and regulations support effective risk management. Reviewing frameworks such as GDPR, PCI DSS and broader information-security frameworks showed me that organisations must select controls according to their specific business activities, information assets, regulatory obligations and risk exposure. Overall, the unit enabled me to better identify applicable regulations and standards, evaluate their relevance to different situations, and recommend proportionate controls and mitigations. I now have a clearer understanding of how frameworks, regulatory requirements and risk management can work together to support secure and responsible digitalisation.
